Facts and Habits

Blue Zones are five geographic regions where people tend to live significantly longer and healthier lives than the global average. Researchers studying these areas identified nine lifestyle principles (often referred to as the Blue Zone “Power 9”) that appear to contribute to this exceptional longevity. The 5 Blue Zones

Click a card to learn more. Hover to pause the carousel.

Okinawa
  • Contains Japan's Shortest River.
  • Karate is an Okinawan export.
  • 36% of Okinawa's Land is Protected.
  • 75% of US Military Bases based in Japan are in Okinawa.
  • Their salt is in the Guinness Book of World Records.
Ikaria
  • Named after Icarus, the Greek mythological figure.
  • Goats roam everywhere and outperform people.
  • Less tourism comparing to other Greek islands.
  • Strong Greek Orthodox traditions shape community life.
  • Traditional “panigiria” (village festivals) are central to life.
Sardinia
  • It is the second largest island in the Mediterranean after Sicily.
  • They have a distinct language (Sardinian).
  • It has one of Europe’s deepest caves.
  • Sardinia’s coastline measures around 1850km long.
Nicoya Peninsula
  • Surf and Yoga culture.
  • Near permanent sunshine.
  • National parks and nature reserves preserving some of the rarest animals in the world.
  • Almost 80 miles of beach along the Pacific Coast.
Loma Linda
  • It is known for its vegan cuisine.
  • It is home to a number of hiking trails.
  • Loma Linda University Medical Center is a leading institution in health research.
  • It has a Mediterranean climate.

Our Approach

What is the goal of this website?

In a society obsessed with finding unnatural ways to extend life, Blue Zones stand out as geographical anomalies where people naturally attain extraordinary longevity. While the nine pillars explain how these centenarians live, we asked ourselves a broader question: are these optimal conditions met in other places around the world?

"We decided to create a Blue Zone Index to identify Turquoise Countries, places that closely mirror these longevity conditions."

Translating qualitative lifestyle habits into global quantitative data is a complex challenge. Among the 9 original pillars, we isolated 5 key indicators that have reliable, measurable data worldwide.

When direct measurements were impossible, we carefully selected statistical proxies. For instance, the pillar of "downshifting" (stress reduction) has been modeled using national happiness levels, allowing us to accurately map this crucial mindset across the globe.
